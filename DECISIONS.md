# Decisions

Dated deviations from, and findings against, `HANDOVER.md`. Newest last. Each line says what changed and why.

## 2026-09-18

- **npm workspaces, not pnpm.** pnpm is not installed on the dev machine and the handover allows either; `npm install` at the root sets up `packages/core` and `apps/web`.
- **Verovio's timemap shape, checked.** Entries are `{ qstamp, tstamp, on, off, restsOn, restsOff, measureOn, tempo }`: `qstamp` is quarter notes (our `q`), `tstamp` milliseconds. Rests are listed under `restsOn`/`restsOff`, not `on`.
- **The app computes its own timeline; Verovio's timemap is a cross-check, not the source.** `buildTimeline` in `packages/core` lays out `q` for every event, and `test/verovio.test.ts` renders the generated MEI with the real Verovio build and asserts that every onset agrees. The cursor and the synth read our timeline; Verovio supplies positions on the page. This keeps timing in tested, framework-free code and means a Verovio upgrade that changes the timemap fails a test instead of the cursor.
- **Two timemap behaviours the app does not copy.** A tied continuation is its own onset in the timemap (we merge ties for playback, and keep the continuation as a cursor step). A note after an unaccented grace note is delayed about 0.03 q by Verovio; our timeline keeps the principal on its beat.
- **MEI for Persian accidentals, checked.** `accid="koron"` / `accid="sori"` on notes, and `<keySig>` with `<keyAccid pname accid>` children for mixed signatures such as B flat, E flat, A koron, render with the SMuFL glyphs U+E460 (koron) and U+E461 (sori).
- **Verovio's MIDI ignores koron:** A koron comes back as MIDI 69, confirming that playback must be ours.
- **Titles are drawn in HTML, not by Verovio.** Verovio does shape Persian text correctly (checked in Chromium), but its page header places the composer awkwardly on wide pages; the app renders with `header: none` and shows the title in the page with `dir="auto"`.
- **Onsets snap to a 1/13440-quarter grid.** Triplet thirds do not add up exactly in floating point, so bar lines drifted to 5.9999999. Every duration the model can express is a multiple of 1/13440 of a quarter (64th x 3 x 5 x 7).
- **A short first measure is a pickup**, not a validation error; its metronome beats are aligned to the bar's end.
- **Grace notes sound before the beat** (75 ms each, nearest first); the cursor stays on the principal.
- **No repeats in the v1 model**, so the timeline's order is already the unfolded order the handover asks `q` to be measured in. Adding repeats means adding an unfold step in `buildTimeline`, and nothing downstream changes.
- **Licence: AGPL-3.0** (the operator's decision). It lifts the handover's interim rule against bundling GPL code: GPL-3.0 (and GPL-2.0-or-later) code may be combined with AGPL-3.0 code, so Rubber Band is an option if the browser's time-stretching proves too poor at low speeds.
- **Test material stays out of the repository.** The operator's test sheets are photographs of published editions (a Ma'rufi radif page and a Shahnazi piece), so `test_sheets/` and `fixtures/private/` are git-ignored. Tests that need them skip when they are absent.
- **Repeats and first/second endings are in the model** (`repeatStart`, `repeatEnd`, `ending` on a measure), because the operator's Mahur test sheet needs them. `buildTimeline` unfolds them, so `q` is measured with repeats unfolded, as the handover asks; an event played twice has two occurrences, and a click on it goes to the one nearest the cursor.
- **Beams are written into the MEI by beat.** Verovio beams only what the MEI groups, and unbeamed eighths and sixteenths are hard to read. Notes shorter than a quarter are beamed within each beat (a dotted quarter in compound meters), and inside a tuplet the whole group is beamed.
- **Editing keys follow MuseScore where the browser allows**: 1–7 durations, A–G letters, `.` dot, `0`/`R` rest, arrows, Ctrl+Z. Two differ: the triplet is Alt+3, since browsers keep Ctrl+3 for switching tabs; koron and sori are K and S. Letters are read from the physical key (`KeyboardEvent.code`), so a Persian keyboard layout types the same notes.
- **Write mode is explicit (N)**, as MuseScore's note input mode: letters add notes after the selection. Outside it, a letter changes the selected note. An empty bar always takes new notes. When the selected note ends a full bar, the next note starts the next bar, which is created if needed, so a melody can be typed from paper without thinking about bar lines.
- **Every edit is a pure function in `packages/core/src/edit.ts`**, tested without a browser; undo keeps whole scores, which at this size costs nothing and cannot drift from the edits.
- **Rhythm words are an HTML layer over the score, not MEI lyrics.** Verovio 6.3 lays out a `<verse>` of Persian syllables right to left across the system, so each syllable lands over the wrong note. The app already measures every note's position for the cursor, so it draws the words itself above each system; they can be shown or hidden without re-rendering and light up with their note during playback. Scope, by the operator's decision: quarter-note-beat meters, one word per beat from the eight-word table; rests, ties, tuplets, half notes and eighth-note meters get no words for now.
- **Slurs and text below the staff are in the model** (`slurTo` on the first note; `below`), because the Abu'ata test sheet needs both. Below-text that is only digits is written as MEI `<fing>`; anything else (such as *rit.*) as a direction. Text above may have several lines, each its own direction, so a gusheh name and a mark can stand over one note. Slurred notes after the first sound softer, as a left-hand note on the tar does without a new stroke.
- **The two test pieces are entered in full** from the operator's pages (Mahur, Shahnazi: 46 bars with repeats and first/second endings; Abu'ata, Ma'rufi: 56 bars with slurs, fingering, T and V marks, حجاز and فرود). They are generated by `fixtures/private/pieces.mjs` and stay local like the photographs. The up-stroke mark is written as a Latin V, which matches the printed glyph.
- **Bar-repeat signs (𝄎), finger lines and double bar lines are in the model**, for the operator's violin exercise (L30Mary). A bar-repeat measure holds no notes: the timeline plays the bar it repeats, and the cursor lights the sign and holds the playhead on it (`display` on the timeline event). A finger line (`lineTo`, key J) is drawn as a straight MEI `<gliss>` and does not change playback. Bow marks (⊓ down, V up), open-string and finger numbers go in the text above the note; the page's small arrows over the bow marks are not reproduced.
- **A bar can carry its own text above** (`text` on the measure, written as a direction at the bar's first beat), for marks with no note under them: the operator's exercise 34 puts bow marks over bar-repeat signs.
- **Fixed: `measureIndexAt` returned a score index where callers expected a play-order index.** Before a repeat the two agree; after one, the position readout and the count-in's meter looked at the wrong bar. It now returns the position in the unfolded list, with a test.
- **The playhead walks through a bar-repeat bar.** Each replayed note is placed in the sign's bar at the same proportion of the bar as it has in the bar being repeated, and the written note it replays is tinted, so the reader sees what is sounding. (Earlier the playhead stood on the sign for the whole bar.)
- **Rests get rhythm words like sounded notes** of the same length (the operator's decision, replacing "no words for rests"): 𝄾 ♪ is می زد, a quarter rest is راست.
- **Note names for beginners** (the operator's request), off by default: Persian solfège (دو ر می … with دیز، بمل، کرن، سری), Do Re Mi, or letters. Each name uses the accidental the note actually sounds with (key signature, an earlier accidental in the bar, a tie), from the timeline; rests, grace notes and tie continuations get none. Drawn under each system like the rhythm words above it, with the accidental stacked under the note name so a name is no wider than its note; it lights up during playback.
- **Plectrum strokes (مضراب راست ∧, مضراب چپ ∨) for tar and setar** (the operator's request). A stroke written on a note (`mezrab`, key M, cycling راست → چپ → none) is notation and always drawn. Where none is written, an optional switch suggests one from the note's place in the beat — راست on the beat and its eighth-note half, چپ on an off-beat sixteenth, nothing under a slur after its first note — drawn lighter. Checked against the Abu'ata page: all ten printed up-strokes fall exactly where the rule suggests چپ. The drum try's "V" and the violin exercises' ⊓/V are bow and other marks and stay text.
- **Spoken note names and rhythm words, generated once and reused** (the operator's request). `tools/voice/generate.py` speaks every note name (Persian solfège, Do Re Mi, letters; with sharp, flat, koron, sori) and each of the eight rhythm words with Piper voices whose data is free to publish (fa_IR-amir, CC0; en_US-ljspeech, public domain), trims and levels each clip, time-stretches it with rubberband to five lengths (0.5×–2×, pitch kept), and packs each set into one MP3 with a JSON index under `apps/web/public/voice/`. The app loads a bank once and, per note, plays the stored length nearest the note (90% of it), nudging the rate by at most −13%/+18% and fading out anything left at the note's end. A name that cannot fit with its accidental is said without it.
- **Rhythm words are spoken whole, one per beat.** Single syllables came out of the voice from 0.06 s to 0.8 s for the same text; whole words are stable, and in Tahmasbi's method a word's syllables already have the notes' lengths, so a word fitted to its beat puts each syllable on its note. Each clip is the best of six takes (speaking rate and variation) by closeness to its expected length.
- **MP3 decoder padding is measured, not assumed**: a bank finds where its first clip's sound begins after decoding and shifts every clip by that amount (46 ms in Chromium).
- **Rhythm words are placed syllable by syllable on the metronome grid** (the operator: "keep in mind which part is needed to be played at the exact metronome grid moment"). A syllable is heard at its vowel onset, not where its sound begins, so that is the grid point: the consonants before it start early. The generator finds each syllable's vowel onset in the spoken word (the n voiced energy nuclei, n known per word; onset halfway up the rise from the valley before), keeps the take whose natural spacing is closest to the word's note pattern, cuts the word at its vowel onsets, stretches each piece to an exact length and joins the pieces with crossfades centred on the target moments — every vowel onset on its sixteenth, at six beat lengths (0.36–1.3 s). rubberband's time map was tried first and drifts early in proportion to the stretch (up to 85 ms); piecewise rendering measures within ±20 ms, mostly ±5 ms, by a tone-burst probe the generator runs on every word.
- **Accent follows the grid**: the syllable on the beat is 2 dB stronger and the syllables between beats 1.5 dB lighter, so the beat is heard as the strong point whatever stress the voice gave the word.
- **At playback the first vowel onset is scheduled on the beat** (each rendering stores its lead-in), from the rendering nearest the real beat length with the rate scaled to match; note names are placed the same way, vowel onset on the note. Checked in Chromium against the metronome's clicks: 0.0 ms at 80 and 60 per minute.
- **Voice colours for the rhythm words** (the operator: "more soft and higher in pitch"), to compare in the app: *natural*, *soft +3* and *soft +5* — pitch raised by 3 or 5 semitones with the formants kept (the same voice higher, not sped up), then softened: the harsh 3–5 kHz presence dipped 4 dB, the top rolled off above 7 kHz, gentle compression, 4 dB quieter. Pitch shifting keeps timing (measured 0–1 ms lag), so a colour shares its source's index and grid. `generate.py --styles-only` re-derives colours without re-synthesizing speech.
- **Correction — the decoder delay.** The "46 ms" measured earlier for the natural bank was mostly the quiet lead-in consonant before the first loud sound, not decoder delay; compression in the soft colours then made the same measurement read 13–21 ms, which would have played soft words late. Each bank now records where its first sound crosses the app's threshold in the audio as encoded (`firstSound`), and the app measures the same crossing after decoding: all three rhythm colours now measure 0–6 ms. Also found on the way: packed banks were 32-bit (sox's default for generated silence) and the generator's reader assumed 16-bit, which only affected this reference; the reader handles both now.
