# M2 — the editor

## What was done, and seen working

- Keyboard-first entry and editing with an on-screen palette mirroring every key (see the Keyboard panel in the app, or `DECISIONS.md`): durations, dots, letters with the nearest octave, chords, rests, koron / sori / flat / sharp / natural, step and octave moves, tie, grace note, triplet, riz, text above a note, delete, add and delete bars, undo and redo.
- A bar panel: clef, time signature or free rhythm, a key signature builder that takes any mix of flats, korons, sharps and soris per step, repeat signs, first and second endings, and how full the bar is. Bars that do not add up are outlined and listed; clicking one selects it.
- A score panel (title, composer, dastgah), tempo and tuning, all undoable.
- Autosave to IndexedDB, restored on reload. New / Open / Save file.
- Every note is heard as it is written or changed.
- Driven in headless Chromium: starting from New, setting 2/4 and typing three bars of the operator's Mahur page with the keyboard produced exactly those notes, flowing across bar lines, with no problems flagged; K on a selected note drew the koron glyph and Ctrl+Z removed it; G changed a note in place; the repeat-back checkbox wrote a repeat; a reload restored the score and its Persian title.
- `packages/core`: 39 tests, including the edit operations and repeat unfolding.

## Added for the two test pieces

- Slurs (L extends a slur note by note, Shift+L pulls it back), text below the staff for fingering,
  several lines of text above one note.
- Tahmasbi's rhythm words over the notes (the «وزن‌خوانی words» switch), for quarter-beat meters.
- Both test pages entered in full and checked against the photographs, bar by bar, from renders of the
  score files: Mahur (46 bars; 140 quarters played with every repeat) and Abu'ata (56 bars). Both open,
  validate with no problems, render with an empty Verovio log, play (A koron at 427.47 Hz in Abu'ata),
  and show 173 and 210 syllables respectively.
- How they were read: at four times the photo's size, straightened, with each notehead measured against
  the nearest staff line. Faint low sixteenths on ledger lines were the hard part: first readings of
  Abu'ata bars 45 and 46 were a step off and were corrected by measuring again. That is what the M5
  review screen will have to catch.
